The buying habit changed

Customers used to type keywords into Google and open ten blue links. Now they ask a question — “find me a BBQ grill,” “which portable power station is quietest” — and the AI answers with a short list. If your product is not in that list, that buyer never sees you. The answer is the new shelf.

Chinese manufacturers and traditional brands feel this first. Your product can be good, well-priced, and well-reviewed — and still invisible to overseas buyers, because the AI never found a page it could cite.

How AI picks a product

An AI answer draws on two sources:

  • What the model remembers. The model was trained on text from the web. It knows the big names. It usually does not know a specific product from a smaller brand — and you cannot pay to be remembered.
  • What the model looks up. For product questions, the AI runs a live search, reads the pages it finds, and writes an answer with citations. This is what shapes product recommendations.

Our work focuses on the second layer: making sure the live search finds your product, reads pages it can use, and has good reason to cite you.

Our process

  1. Audit. Measure where you stand now, so later results can be compared.
  2. Technical fixes. Open the site to AI crawlers, add structured data, fix indexing.
  3. Content. Publish pages that answer real customer questions in a citable form.
  4. Third-party mentions. Earn coverage where AI answers look for evidence.
  5. Monitoring. Track mention and citation rates weekly, and adjust.

An example

Someone asks ChatGPT: “find me a BBQ grill.” ChatGPT rewrites the question into search queries, searches Bing’s index and its own index, opens the top pages, reads prices, specs, and reviews, and writes a short list with links. A grill gets recommended only if its pages were found, read, and judged useful. A grill with no pages, no prices, and no reviews in the index is very unlikely to be picked.

Questions, answered briefly

Can you guarantee my product will be recommended?
No. No one can — OpenAI itself says placement is not guaranteed. What we do is raise the probability, measure it, and show you the numbers.

How long until we see results?
Brand-name questions can show up within a few weeks once pages are indexed, depending on competition and indexing. Competitive generic questions often take months. We set expectations per question, not per promise.

How is this different from SEO?
Same foundations, different surface. SEO fights for a place in a list of links. GEO fights for a place inside the answer itself.
